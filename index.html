02 — How it works

The test-and-trust layer around any robot AI

We don't build robots or AI models. We're the neutral, plant-side check, so the model maker doesn't grade its own homework.

  1. 01 · Test

    Measure the true success rate

    Run the robot's AI hundreds of times in simulation under changed conditions: lighting, object position, delays. Get the success rate with a confidence interval, plus the types of failure.

  2. 02 · Guard

    Catch failures before they happen

    At runtime, a failure alarm watches for signs the AI is confused and fires before a visible failure. A hard safety envelope (speed limits, workspace limits, human-zone rules) slows or stops the robot.

  3. 03 · Prove

    Hand over the evidence

    One click produces a Deployment Readiness Report that a plant's safety lead can put in their risk assessment.

  • Model-agnostic
  • Hardware-agnostic
  • Neutral and plant-side

05 — Why Cincinnati

Robot brains are built on the coasts. The risk lands here.

Capital and model labs cluster in the Bay Area and Pittsburgh, but deployment risk lands in the Midwest. Ohio's manufacturing sector supports more than 687,000 jobs and ranks 3rd in the U.S. for manufacturing employment (Ohio Manufacturers' Association, opens in a new tab). When automation doesn't deliver, the cost lands here too: Cincinnati-based Kroger expected about $2.6 billion in charges as it closed three robotic fulfillment centers (Chain Store Age, opens in a new tab).

The Midwest doesn't need to build the next robot brain. It needs to be able to check one before trusting it on the line.

07 — FAQ

Questions we get

Are you building a robot AI model?

No. We build on open models (like Hugging Face SmolVLA and Physical Intelligence's π0) and make them testable and guardable.

Can't the AI companies test their own models?

They do, on their own benchmarks. But a plant running robots from several vendors needs a neutral yardstick, tested on its own parts, lighting and line. Same logic as UL listings and third-party crash tests.

Is Proofline a safety certification?

No. It provides evidence for a plant's risk assessment and works alongside certified safety hardware, not instead of it.
